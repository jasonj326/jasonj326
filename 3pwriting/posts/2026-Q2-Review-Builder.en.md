## 5 · Five-Star Finds {#s5}

1. [Sun Brand Ice (太陽牌冰品)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/2aXo8XsWzuDEPthN7): a mid-May trip to Tainan brought me to this old-school ice treat — the most recommendable bite of the whole trip. After all my visits to Tainan, all I can say is: we met far too late.
2. [Wang's Fucheng Rice Dumplings (王記府城肉粽)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/au1h3kGXm6QPoKeF7): this one hardly needs my endorsement — Jensen has autographed the place three times. One useful note: dine-in barely queues (we went on Dragon Boat Festival day and were seated within five minutes).
3. [Arashiyama Aged Gyukatsu (嵐山熟成牛かつ專売)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/amWQ6FLTk2XpFJxN9): finding this place was mildly surreal. I was headed to Taichung, restless on the HSR, with a gap before an event and no idea where to eat or rest. So I quit searching and went on instinct. Near the venue I walked into a brunch café with exactly one table of customers. "Sit anywhere," they said. I headed for the second floor — "the second floor isn't open." Fine, a window seat then — "window seats are reserved for larger parties." Hmm — why won't you just seat me? "It's fine, I'll come back some other time."
	I walked out, and there stood this gyukatsu shop. Big bites of beef, far beyond expectation. "Whatever happens, treat it as the best thing that could have happened right now."
4. [Morris Chang's autobiography](https://www.books.com.tw/products/0011005571): I haven't finished the second volume yet, but reading it leaves me wanting more. What comes through isn't just a business titan — it's the warmth of a human being.
5. [Talk Less, Win More: How Lawyers in Business Development Roles Can Turn Meetings Into Revenue](https://www.amazon.com/Talk-Less-Win-More-Development-ebook/dp/B0H24FYXH1/?utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email): I've been meaning to write a proper review and never started. In early 2025 it struck me that fresh out of college I should have done sales to sharpen my tongue — business school calls it marketing, lawyers call it advocacy, same essence — but street-smart selling, whether 2B or 2C, is only forged in the field. This book is by Alex, a lawyer who moved into sales, writing for legal people. I finished it itching to try.

Honestly, I'm still not sure how to cleanly separate "Four on Repeat" from "Five-Star Finds."
For now, Four is the artsy shelf — poetry and faraway places — while Five is the practical one: self-improvement, or food and the appetites. Though I've discovered I don't carry that much artistic nourishment anyway. Either way: whatever is worth sharing and recommending gets recorded.

## 6 · Six on a Roll: six habits and sparks {#s6}

1. Build in public: this is the quarter's theme in detail — inspired from many directions: *Show Your Work*, the spirit of early open source and Web3's culture of openness[^1], and last quarter's builder-mindset training. I wanted my own practice and my building out in the open too. Beyond a public GitHub, there's the livestream diary, these new quarterly reviews, and Japanese study picked back up after months of neglect. This stage is close to what Alex Hormozi describes in ["How to (logically) catch up in life"](https://youtu.be/EonibwnAEME?si=WEXuqrnQCeBzwz30) — building capacity. Don't know what to do? Sleep, lift, read, experiment, ask people who know, volunteer for the experience. It hit home this quarter when a new friend invited me out and I had to decline because my insomnia had worsened — that's what insufficient capacity feels like: not enough health in the tank. And why do it publicly? Because in an ocean of people and an explosion of information, practice that is transparent is easier to trust.
2. The morning playlist: a new habit since roughly the start of Q2 — giving the morning a small ritual instead of reaching for the phone on waking. I manage four or five days out of seven. Keep building; maybe rotate the playlist every six months for freshness.
	https://open.spotify.com/playlist/4DV0QJTmgCL4sRnVe3sSuU?si=QI1pDr-wQPGsj8hghM2TLQ
3. Growing my hair out — nearly six months without a haircut now. New experience, new habit. The current plan: keep it until forty. Let's see if it survives this summer.
4. Practicing analogy, metaphor, and simile: I've always wanted to train the knack of reaching for the example close at hand — a truly creative analogy makes me slap the table — but I could never find the way in. Then recently, through AI, I learned "forced association": weight training for the brain, and perhaps, at last, my entry-level program.
5. A new input method — voice-flow dictation. Honestly I can't articulate how it differs from old-school voice input; probably that AI now corrects your words at speed. The main players are Typeless and Wispr. I'm not used to it yet — but when a flow state hits, it's wings on a tiger.

## 7 · Seven Fast Failures {#s7}

A log of the setbacks and the slumps.

1. Coach bot goes on break: I had built an assistant-slash-ADD/ADHD-coach out of the Anthropic API + Cloudflare D1 + a Telegram bot + Google Sheets. Its mission: reach out proactively four or five times a day, log my daily minutiae, and coach me to stay on what matters. The difficulties were many. First, logging must be deterministic, while coaching with emotional intelligence needs to be stochastic — and the bot's judgment was still weak, erred constantly, and debugging burned serious money. Second, I had to feed it my own progress reports — and when I'm busy, I have zero bandwidth for that. Starved of input, both the coach and the assistant turn useless. So it's on break for now. Stop burning money.
2. PIF12 never submitted its Emergent Ventures application: self-limiting, plus not yet knowing how to tell the story.
3. Still too much time scrolling social media — but I did compile the valuable, interesting things I found in Q2, for anyone curious[^2]
4. Attempting atomic habits and a digital filing system: after a friend's introduction and an earlier course, I wanted my information flow sorted more automatically. The plan said tidy every five days; a month and a half evaporated, and I only started sorting in early July, while writing this review.
5. Insomnia: it worsened in Q2. In May, during the Ministry of Health and Welfare's young-adult counseling program, my sleep seemed to be turning a corner — then June fell off a cliff: either I can't fall asleep, or I wake in the small hours.
6. The leaky house: old house, old problems. All of 2024 went into repairs; 2025 finally seemed settled — then June it leaked again. Mercifully fixed in early July. The lesson? Go make more money, faster.
7. Making peace with rejected SBTs: PIF12 launched onchain on the summer solstice, June 21, and I sent tokens to friends — a few seemed uninterested, and my glass heart cracked a little. The lessons: [practice speaking kindly to yourself](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DZshivLDyf3/), count each rejection as game experience earned, don't over-read someone not claiming a gift — and revisit the virtue of ["I love you, and it has nothing to do with you"](https://jasonjlai.net/seeds/#2021-01-02-mrps002).

## 8 · Eight Pillars: where my focus went in Q2 {#s8}

1. The AI fleet: mainly Claude and Gemini, every day. AI tools feel like teenage romance — everyone knows the breakup is only a few years out, yet that first-love, first-mover thrill is irresistible. In early July I had Fable audit my own AI usage; the details are [here](https://jasonjlai.net/3pwriting/playbooks/ai-fleet-audit.html).
2. The website: after browsing many blogrolls ([for instance](https://wiwi.blog/blogroll)), my favorite Q2 addition is the [seed wall](https://jasonjlai.net/seeds/) — may it grow into a garden in full bloom. Also refreshed the gamified-thinking pages. The self-training continues.
3. Markets × escaping to wealth: the two most tiresome small-talk topics of the moment are stocks and AI — and I can't stand myself, because I open with both every time. Most people will never actually buy Treasuries, but if you hold fiat in a bank you already hold bonds: you have lent the bank your money, and "deposit" is simply the word game for that IOU. Cash and bonds usually swing less than stocks — but these past two years, especially in Taiwan, everyone is a stock god. No one knows when the market corrects or where the bubble sits. Mr. Market's moods are his own; serving him is like attending a tiger, walking on thin ice. Earn while you can — and treasure it.

## 9 · Nine Lives: nine comfort-zone tests {#s9}

From harder to easier.

1. Three talks in English (challenge: medium) — the Blockchain Lawyers Forum, plus hosting two TCE sessions. As of July 7 I'm at six talks for the year, still a distance from the 24-talk goal. Onward — [invitations of every kind welcome](https://jasonjlai.net/communication/).
2. Going live from the HSR platform (challenge: medium): one episode of the livestream diary began right on the high-speed-rail platform, travelers packed around me — genuinely uncomfortable. I kept thinking of [this video](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DZX0vP3tjLS/) and [this one](https://www.instagram.com/p/DWILg8-uwbk/).
3. Formatting the quarterly review (challenge: medium). This is probably the format from here on — holding at fifty-five items. Next improvement: borrow from IPO prospectuses and add more forward-looking hopes and expectations.
4. Deploying the smart contract, onchain on schedule (challenge: medium-low)
5. Introducing PIF12 to new friends (challenge: medium-low)
6. Joining the ETHTaipei organizing team (challenge: medium-low)
7. Serving as an interviewer for a club (challenge: medium-low)
8. In-person meetups of online communities × 2: Waki's community and Manny's (challenge: low)
9. Growing my hair long; joining an amateur makeover event in Taichung (challenge: low)

"Nine Lives" is a new idea that came out of rethinking the review format. Five levels of challenge: high (serious money, reputation, and time at stake), medium-high (money, reputation, and time), medium (reputation and time), medium-low (reputation or time), low (a novel experience without much risk). Here's hoping Q3 carries more high and medium-high entries.
